October 23, 2026 · Engineering field notes

From test tone to unattended MTC: first 11-question bench session

On a Windows desktop using free Python software, VEIL assembled a preliminary MTC playback-and-recording setup with Realtek speakers and a HyperX SoloCast USB microphone. The development sequence confirmed an audible test tone, captured known speech and a handclap, then progressed from a single prerecorded question to an eleven-question unattended session.

The 11-question session ran approximately 5 minutes 27 seconds, with 15 seconds after each prompt for listening. The application finished automatically; the researcher subsequently listened to the saved room-microphone WAV and confirmed that the spoken prompts were audible. The integrity-review utility reported PASS_WITH_MANUAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED; manual acoustic playback review was performed, but the results have not undergone independent verification.

What this establishes: A first physical bench prototype can play prerecorded prompts and save an unattended acoustic recording in the tested Windows configuration. What it does not establish: unusual voices, communication with an external intelligence, long-duration reliability, the effectiveness of controls, or proof of paranormal phenomena.

Next engineering steps: preserve original files and logs, repeat trials with known controls, test longer segment transitions, corroborate recording with an independent device, and preregister blinded analysis criteria before attempting substantive EVP research.

October 12, 2026 · Community announcement

Curious about the unexplained? So are we.

VEIL Research Project has opened its official Facebook Page as a welcoming place to exchange questions, personal experiences and curiosity about how unusual claims can be tested. Believers, skeptics and those still making up their minds are equally welcome, with no ridicule or harassment.

We'll explore the history of unusual paranormal instruments and experiments: what was claimed, what was actually tested, and what the evidence did—or did not—show. We also hope to develop accessible, safe at-home research activities with proper controls. Personal stories deserve respectful hearing, but they are not automatically scientific evidence.

The VEIL website remains our record for proposed protocols, project status, limitations and methods. We are not claiming paranormal proof or a completed research laboratory.

October 10, 2026 · Research integrity policy

What would make us change our minds?

Before investing in an experimental program, VEIL now asks four questions: what previous studies found, whether the physical mechanism is defensible, which outcomes can be measured against proper controls, and what results would require abandonment or redesign.

We have provisionally prioritized MTC, Temperature Curtain and Making the Invisible Visible for methodological development. Panic Box and Perfect Storm are engineering platforms. ECHO and GB-001 remain exploratory proposals deferred until stronger scientific questions can be stated. This classification is not independent scientific approval.

Informative null results, calibration failures and methodological limitations would be documented rather than hidden. VEIL has not yet published physical results.

October 9, 2026 · Project note

Why observer-free EVP testing matters

When someone suggests a phrase in an ambiguous recording, other listeners may hear the same words. MTC proposes recording without a human observer in the monitored area and reviewing the audio afterward using blinded listening and control sessions. The goal is to separate actual recorded signals from interpretation and expectation.

VEIL has not yet demonstrated an anomalous EVP source. The immediate questions are how to isolate ordinary noise, record reproducible controls and evaluate listener agreement without prompting.
