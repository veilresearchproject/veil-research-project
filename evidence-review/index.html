What if your evidence deserved a second look?

VEIL is exploring the idea of an Independent Evidence Review program: a place where investigators could request a careful, neutral second opinion on reported anomalous audio, video, photographs and instrument data.

We're asking for feedback, not accepting cases. This is an early proposal. There is no operational review center, certified evidence-clearinghouse, open submission portal or paid review program today.

A collaborative second opinion

Not “prove it’s a ghost.” Understand what the evidence can and cannot show.

A good review would preserve the original evidence, document how it was recorded, check for ordinary explanations and state the limits of any conclusion. An unresolved observation would remain unresolved—not automatically “paranormal.”

1 · Preserve

Ask for original files and collection context when possible; keep originals untouched, track copies and document provenance.

2 · Examine

Check metadata, timing, environmental factors, artifacts, alternative explanations and whether the claimed event survives reasonable controls.

3 · Review

Use clearly documented methods, human oversight and a second reviewer where feasible. SIGNAL could eventually assist with preliminary flags, but would not decide the outcome.

4 · Report

Explain observations, tests, uncertainty, limitations and recommended next steps. Findings would be descriptive, not certificates of supernatural authenticity.

We want both believers and skeptics at the table

What would make an independent review useful to you?

Before we build a pilot, we'd like to hear from field teams, individual investigators, technical reviewers and people who have had unusual experiences.

The questions

Would you submit evidence for a second opinion? What types of files matter most? What should a useful report include? How could we make the process fair, private and credible?

The safeguards

Reviewers would need clear conflict-of-interest rules, permission to analyze submissions, privacy and retention controls, reliable original-file handling, and a process for correcting errors.

Early-stage roadmap

Free pilot first. Paid services only if the methods earn trust.

A possible first step is a limited, voluntary, no-fee pilot using consented test cases and published evaluation criteria. We would measure consistency, accuracy, reviewer time and usefulness before considering any fees. No prices, accreditations or launch dates have been set.

The guiding principle: If paid reviews are eventually offered, the fee would cover the review process—not purchase a desired conclusion.

VEIL is an independent research initiative. A staffed physical research center and formal evidence review operations are long-term goals, not existing services.
